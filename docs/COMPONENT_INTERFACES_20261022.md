## One next engineering question

Can a selected, independently supported shaft interface permit the **same defined service task** while preserving rotor/generator datums, without requiring adjacent-machine withdrawal?

**Design hypothesis:** separating component renewal from cartridge extraction may reduce disassembly and realignment burden compared with a one-piece interface. Necessary evidence: qualified load cases, shaft/key geometry, torsional review, support in every service state, supplier-qualified environment/fit, dimensioned removal motion, matched task timing and complete installed costs. Falsifier: required support or access does not fit, or added hardware, water-system and service burdens erase any measured benefit.

**Manufacturing tradeoffs, engineering inferences:** split housings, matched hub parts, precision bores and replaceable sleeves can add machining, inspection, inventory and alignment interfaces. Avoiding removal of larger assemblies may reduce some service work, but neither labour savings nor payback is known. A wet bearing’s cooling/conditioning, monitoring and piping belong in the BOM and energy boundary. Meter actual auxiliary demand and avoid double counting; zero auxiliary power is not implied by water lubrication.

**Do not confuse bearing retention with rotor support.** A retaining shoulder/ring is not a documented rotor-thrust capacity. Radial and axial rotor support, including a vertical rotor’s service-state support, must be independently engineered. No disassembly or lift procedure is supplied.
