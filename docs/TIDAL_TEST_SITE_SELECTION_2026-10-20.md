# TT-SITES-001 — Nova Scotia tidal-stream generator TEST LOCATIONS (2026-10-20)

**Purpose:** Find real in-water locations for testing, measuring, retrieving and iterating tidal-stream generator prototypes within plausible reach of Halifax. This is **not a coastal land purchase ranking, a grid electricity business case or a turbine-performance certification**. The 2018 Acadia/OERA study is historical source evidence, not proof of a 2026 authorization, a working public berth or a current tidal velocity at a particular point.

## Decision: different locations for different stages

**First close-to-Halifax location to investigate remotely:** **Rocky Run Bridge / Porters Lake inlet at Lawrencetown, HRM.** The 2018 small-tidal-test-centre study (§3.4.2.5, report p.25) identifies an historical 1985 turbine test at Rocky Run and visually estimates a peak flow of at least **2 m/s**, perhaps **3 m/s**. **This is not an instrument-measured velocity, annual current characterization, permitted berth, rotor depth or safety assessment.** The study did **not** carry Rocky Run forward into its formal test-centre site comparison. Source: [Acadia/OERA 2018 test-centre gap analysis](https://netzeroatlantic.ca/sites/default/files/2019-05/Nova%20Scotia%20Small%20Tidal%20Test%20Centre%20-%20Gap%20Analysis%20and%20Business%20Case.pdf). Community-sourced bridge point is around 44.64386 N, 63.31414 W [Mapcarta/OpenStreetMap](https://mapcarta.com/W541133687); this is a bridge map reference, **not a turbine deployment coordinate**.

**Best documented established open-water mid-stage candidate:** **Grand Passage between Freeport and Westport.** The 2018 study (§3.4.2.2, report pp.21-22; appendix report p.54) characterizes 2–3 m/s regional screening flows, ~10–15 m and ~30 m depth environments, and access from wharves/harbours during all tidal phases. Floating turbines and mooring research were historically conducted there (not proof that a public berth exists or old equipment remains). See [NRCan hydrodynamic research summary](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/funding-partnerships/reducing-cost-stream-tidal-energy-generation-through-comprehensive-hydrodynamic-site-assessment), [Sustainable Marine's project history](https://www.sustainablemarine.com/grand-passage), and [2024 independently published wake observations](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S096014812400380X). Wildlife, fisheries, ferries, in-water permissions and berth access remain gating constraints.

**Later stronger-current test site:** **Petit Passage between East Ferry and Tiverton.** Same 2018 report screens roughly 2.5–4.5 m/s flow areas (and describes peaks around 4–5 m/s), medium-depth (~30–50 m) environments with wharf access. A 2012 ADCP dataset underlies local resource studies; that report's observations must be matched to exact device depth, site and dates before quoting yield. [NRCan site study](https://natural-resources.canada.ca/funding-partnerships/reducing-cost-stream-tidal-energy-generation-through-comprehensive-hydrodynamic-site-assessment); [FORCE Petit Passage ADCP-calibrated study](https://fundyforce.ca/document-collection/characterizing-tidal-flows-and-turbine-power-production-in-petit-passage-using-oceanographic-and-cfd-models-). As of a current [Tethys project entry](https://tethys.pnnl.gov/project-sites/nova-innovation-nova-tidal-array), the Nova Innovation first turbine is **planned for 2027**, not demonstrated operating in 2026. Its project authorization confers **no access to us**.

**Extreme-load late-stage option:** **Minas Passage / FORCE, Black Rock.** This is a grid-connected, instrumented research and tidal-turbine berth facility, not a casual low-budget test pool. The 2018 study (§3.4.2.1, report pp.18–20) notes extreme flow/turbulence, limited subsea optical visibility and narrow tide-limited dry-port access, despite the established research base. [FORCE](https://fundyforce.ca/about-us) and [2018 ADCP current metadata](https://cioosatlantic.ca/erddap/info/FORCE_Mar2018_ADCP_Currents/index.html). The existing repository currently has metadata and QA tooling but **has not ingested instrument-vetted, rotor-depth site time-series**. Current berth availability, access fees and permission for a new small demonstrator remain unknown.

**Additional current-test candidate:** **Digby Gut** is a 2–3 m/s *localized* screening flow with relatively easy access to Digby harbour, but greater ferry/marine traffic and lower water clarity; fish migration and acoustic detection are important. It is a fallback to Grand Passage only if operator safety, test access and actual rotor-height velocity comparison favor it. [2018 report](https://netzeroatlantic.ca/sites/default/files/2019-05/Nova%20Scotia%20Small%20Tidal%20Test%20Centre%20-%20Gap%20Analysis%20and%20Business%20Case.pdf) §3.4.2.3 and appendix p.54. Indian Sluice Bridge in Argyle is another exploratory shallow-water option, with a historical maximum **4.1 m/s surface GPS drifter** speed—not a rotor-hub measurement—so lower priority given distance and unknown access (same report §3.4.2.5).

**Non-tidal proving facility, explicitly excluded from stream energy yield ranking:** [COVE Stella Maris](https://covesolutions.com/programs-services/stella-maris-testing-solution/) in Dartmouth provides subsea sensor/dockside testing, a shoreside operations centre and real-time data. It may suit submersion, seals, control electronics, telemetry and recoverability trials **only after COVE approves the exact apparatus**. No published moving-rotor authorization or suitable tidal velocity is established. This is a supplementary laboratory/wet-integration step, *not* a candidate for tidal electricity claims.

## Stage-gated screening

| Stage | Actual engineering question | Preferred candidate | Evidence gate / stop condition |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0 — dry/wet integration | Can the rotor, seals, bearings, electronics, sensors, shutdown/retrieval interface and load bank function safely in salt water? | Bench/flume; optional COVE for **non-tidal** wet work | Demonstrate safe isolation, sealing, calibrated instrumentation and emergency stop; operator must accept moving machinery |
| 1 — small current-driven first field test | Can a removable prototype survive and produce measurable net electricity during ebb/flood without trapping debris or obstructing fish/navigation? | Rocky Run **remote reconnaissance**, then instrumented study **only with consent** | Qualified ADCP/profile flow data (m/s vs time/depth), low-water/swept-area clearance, reversible mounting plan, infrastructure/permissions; if any gate fails, **no installation** |
| 2 — medium flow research | Does measured net output and loading match simulation, and is safe repeated retrieval feasible? | Grand Passage; Digby Gut as comparator | Named berth/current permissions, matched measured flow, marine vessel/recovery plan, independent monitoring, seasonal availability and cost quotation |
| 3 — demanding validated tests | Does the device/control maintain safe loads, lifetime energy and ecosystem monitoring across high flow? | Petit Passage, then FORCE | Independent fatigue/load validation, DFO/NS/Transport permissions, granted berth/site rights, design-load envelope, monitoring and decommission plan |

These stages are **development hypotheses**, not legally exempt power categories. Specific device size/capacity, mounting, storage/disposal of output, in-water duration and whether it exports to grid all affect permissions.

## Safety, regulatory and access gates — do not bypass

- **Do not attach equipment to a bridge, put cables in navigable water, deploy moorings or create in-water obstructions without project-specific permissions.** Rocky Run is mapped as a provincial bridge; title/operator rights, public road safety and DFO/Transport/provincial watercourse requirements are not established. Fish passage and users of the inlet are explicit decision constraints.
- **Historical permission is not transferable.** A [2018 provincial Grand Passage non-grid trial permit](https://news.novascotia.ca/en/2018/09/19/tidal-permit-issued-black-rock-tidal-power) allowed another developer's 280 kW floating test for six months with environmental monitoring, proving an off-grid precedent **but not** an exemption for a new prototype.
- Nova Scotia's [2026 tidal demonstration application window](https://novascotia.ca/programs/tidal-demonstration-permit/) ends **Oct 30, 2026 at 11:59pm**, for projects **below 2 MW** within a designated **Fundy priority area outside FORCE**, with grid power-purchase arrangements. Whether a small non-grid bridge/tether demonstration qualifies is **unknown**, not presumed.
- Fishing, marine mammal/fish ecology, Mi'kmaw rights and engagement, navigation, seabed/bridge land rights, emergency retrieval and permit costs are **go/no-go conditions**, not checklist scores that can be traded away for fast flow.

## High-value next actions, prioritized to tidal-stream testing (no property acquisition)

1. **Rocky Run desktop check:** trace bridge custodian, bathymetry/low-water clearance, historical 1985/1987 test geometry and public hydrodynamic data. If no depth-specific velocity observations exist, design a bounded, reversible **non-deployment measurement plan** for approval; do not fabricate 2026 current speed from 2018 visual estimates.
2. **Grand Passage benchmark dossier:** retrieve raw/data-table ADCP studies and 2024 wake paper's dataset; register exact position/vertical datum, time/QC/flood-ebb and corresponding existing platform locations. Compare only observed **at matched physical conditions**. Build a shortlist of actual qualified marine operators/hosts and access costs; no contact until user authorization.
3. **Device-dependent site match:** define expected rotor diameter, mass, rated speed, output dump/load, retrieval arrangement, service vessel and whether grid export is intended. These are prerequisites to selecting a site or estimating an affordable test.
4. **Permission and ecosystem matrix:** itemize NS/DFO/Transport/bridge owner, fishing and Mi'kmaw engagement requirements for Rocky Run and Grand Passage. Record evidence and approval state; not assumed granted.
5. **No land purchase:** use existing marine access or a rented workshop if useful. A lot near a powerful strait does not grant turbine test rights; only consider shore acquisition after an actual chosen test location, permit path and verified vessel/retrieval logistics.
